Key concepts

• Discontinuous conduction: the inductor current reaches zero for part of each cycle.

• Continuous conduction: the inductor current remains above zero, although it may contain ripple.

• For a single-phase rectifier, there are two output-voltage pulses per supply cycle.

• For a three-phase six-pulse rectifier, there are six DC-side output-voltage pulses per supply cycle.

• The required number of pulses depends on whether the waveform is on the AC/source side or the DC/rectifier-output side.

• A capacitive DC output can appear nearly constant, with very small ripple if the capacitance is large.

• An inductively smoothed output should still show some ripple unless the idealisation explicitly implies otherwise.

• Three-phase rectifiers produce an output waveform closer to constant DC than single-phase rectifiers.

• Increasing the supply frequency while leaving the filter components unchanged moves the input further above the filter cutoff frequency, increasing attenuation and reducing ripple.

• The three-phase rectifier source current is closer to a sinusoid than the single-phase source current in the examples shown, so its THD is expected to be lower.

• LTspice simulation results must be allowed to reach steady state before being used as the basis for a sketch.

• The current probe direction matters. A probe may display the negative of the intended current depending on its orientation.

• A three-phase rectifier has six pulses per fundamental cycle on the DC side, corresponding to a sixth-harmonic ripple component.
