02 · BUILD THE MODEL

Main concepts

  • Project management organises work to deliver agreed results within time, cost, quality, scope, and resource constraints.
  • Project management is a people-oriented engineering activity involving collaboration, communication, stakeholder understanding, and translating technical ideas into reality.
  • The project-management iron triangle describes trade-offs among scope, cost, time, and sometimes quality.
  • PMBOK was presented through initiation, planning, execution, monitoring and control, and closing. PRINCE2 was contrasted as a more continuously changing and controlled approach.
  • Stakeholder work begins at project initiation and continues through planning, delivery, and review.
  • An interest-power or interest-influence chart helps prioritise stakeholder engagement.
  • A work breakdown structure identifies and decomposes the work required to deliver a project. It initially focuses on what must be done, not when, how, by whom, or at what cost.
  • Task sequencing follows work decomposition and uses finish-to-start, start-to-start, finish-to-finish, and start-to-finish dependencies.
  • Activity durations should account for judgement, previous projects, resources, procurement lead times, uncertainty, constraints, and possible delays.
  • Monitoring compares actual progress with the plan. Significant deviations may require formal change control and approval.
  • Risk arises from uncertainty and may affect technical performance, safety, the environment, finances, reputation, people, property, schedules, or project objectives.
  • Risk management is broader than risk assessment. Risk assessment consists of identification, analysis, and evaluation; risk management also includes context-setting, treatment, ownership, communication, monitoring, review, and documentation.
  • Risk identification should distinguish the cause or source, the risk event, and its consequences.
  • Risk criteria and thresholds should be established before risks are scored.
  • Risk treatment may reduce likelihood, reduce consequence, share or transfer risk, or accept the remaining risk.
  • Residual risk is the risk remaining after treatment. Decisions should be based on residual risk, not only initial risk.
  • Formal procedures can fail when organisational culture, incentives, communication, governance, disclosure, or independent oversight are inadequate.
  • The Boeing 737 MAX case illustrated interacting technical, commercial, training, regulatory, organisational, and governance risks.

03 · WORK THE MECHANICS

Equations and worked patterns

  • Qualitative project-constraint pattern:
  • Change in one project constraint → likely change or trade-off in another constraint.
  • Risk score:
  • Risk score = likelihood score × consequence score
  • Residual risk score:
  • Residual risk score = post-treatment likelihood × post-treatment consequence
  • Project planning sequence:
  • Define objective and deliverables → create the WBS → sequence tasks → estimate durations and contingency → create the schedule or Gantt chart → monitor actual progress → assess deviations → apply approved changes.
  • Risk-management sequence:
  • Establish context → identify causes, events, and consequences → analyse likelihood and consequence → evaluate against predefined criteria → treat the risk → reassess residual risk → assign ownership → monitor, review, and document.
  • Dependency patterns:
  • FS: Task B starts after Task A finishes.
  • SS: Task B starts after Task A starts.
  • FF: Task B finishes after Task A finishes.
  • SF: Task B finishes after Task A starts.
  • The lectures did not provide a universal numerical equation for the iron triangle, a universal residual-risk threshold, or a complete FMEA formula.
