02 · BUILD THE MODEL

Main concepts

  • An organisation’s employment cost exceeds an employee’s salary because it may include employer contributions, ACC-related costs, and other benefits. Annual leave, public holidays, and non-billable activities reduce the hours available for revenue-generating work. [Lecture 17, Estimating employment cost; Utilisation and billable hours]
  • Utilisation is the proportion of available working time spent on billable work. Charge-out rates must recover employment costs, non-billable time, overheads, operating costs, and profit. [Lecture 17, Utilisation and billable hours; Charge-out rates]
  • Sunk costs are already incurred and unrecoverable. They should not influence forward-looking decisions, while opportunity cost is the benefit forgone by not choosing the next-best alternative. [Lecture 17, Sunk costs; Opportunity cost]
  • Top-down costing is fast and experience-based but may be biased or insufficiently traceable. Bottom-up costing decomposes the project into tasks and adds direct costs, indirect costs, contingency, and profit. [Lecture 17, Top-down costing; Bottom-up costing]
  • Cost estimates become more detailed and reliable as a project develops, but unexpected events and omitted costs can still create errors. [Lecture 17, Staged estimates for large projects]
  • The income statement moves from revenue through direct costs, operating expenditure, depreciation and amortisation, interest, and tax to net profit. [Lecture 18, Income statement; EBITDA, EBIT, and net profit]
  • Profit is not the same as cash. A profitable company may have little cash, while a company reporting a loss may still have substantial cash reserves. [Lecture 18, Losses, cash, and business condition; Profit versus cash]
  • The cash-flow statement separates operating, investing, and financing activities. [Lecture 18, Cash-flow statement]
  • Financial performance should be assessed using multiple statements, ratios, and time periods rather than one isolated measure. [Lecture 18, Overview; Horizontal analysis and financial trends]
  • The sustainability assignment rewards a defensible process: explicit assumptions, justified boundaries and data choices, evidence, stakeholder consideration, and a clear conclusion. [Lecture 18, Sustainability assignment guidance; Stakeholders and assignment conclusions]

03 · WORK THE MECHANICS

Equations and worked patterns

  • Total employment cost = gross salary + employer contributions + levies + other employee benefits. [Lecture 17, Equations and definitions]
  • Utilisation rate = billable hours / total available working hours. [Lecture 17, Equations and definitions]
  • Billable hours = total annual working hours × utilisation rate.
  • Lecture example: 1,600 hours × 80% = approximately 1,280 billable hours. [Lecture 17, Utilisation and billable hours]
  • Employment cost per billable hour = total employment cost / billable hours.
  • Lecture example: $85,000 / approximately 1,280 hours = approximately $66 per billable hour. [Lecture 17, Equations and definitions]
  • Estimated price = direct costs + indirect costs + contingency + profit margin. [Lecture 17, Bottom-up costing]
  • Gross profit = revenue − cost of goods sold.
  • EBITDA = gross profit − operating expenditure.
  • EBIT = EBITDA − depreciation − amortisation.
  • Profit before tax = EBIT − interest.
  • Net profit = profit before tax − tax. [Lecture 18, Income-statement relationships]
  • Gross profit margin = gross profit / revenue.
  • EBITDA margin = EBITDA / revenue.
  • EBIT margin = EBIT / revenue.
  • Net profit margin = net profit after tax / revenue. [Lecture 18, Profitability margins]
  • Return on total assets = EBIT / total assets.
  • Return on equity = net profit after tax / total equity. [Lecture 18, Return ratios]
  • Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities.
  • Debt-to-equity ratio = debt / equity, subject to the course convention for defining debt. [Lecture 18, Liquidity and leverage]
  • Net change in cash = net operating cash flow + net investing cash flow + net financing cash flow.
  • Closing cash = opening cash + net change in cash. [Lecture 18, Cash-flow relationship]
