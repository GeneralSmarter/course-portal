02 · BUILD THE MODEL

Main concepts

  • Engineers are data custodians because they design and operate systems that collect, store, process, transfer, and protect personal information.
  • Data privacy concerns how personal information is collected, stored, accessed, used, corrected, transferred, and disclosed.
  • Data ethics requires consideration of privacy, transparency, bias, discrimination, accountability, autonomy, fairness, and consequences.
  • Data collection benefits must be balanced against risks to privacy, security, fairness, justice, and trust.
  • Privacy and security failures can cause identity theft, fraud, reputational damage, regulatory penalties, litigation, and substantial financial loss.
  • APIs require effective authentication, authorisation, and protection against enumeration and object-level access-control failures.
  • NDAs define confidential information, permitted uses and disclosures, protection requirements, consequences of breach, and the duration of confidentiality obligations.
  • Confidentiality obligations may also arise from professional codes, even without an NDA.
  • The New Zealand Privacy Act 2020 was discussed in relation to collection purpose, notice, security, access, correction, retention, disclosure, offshore transfers, and breach reporting. Exact statutory requirements require checking current official sources.
  • The GDPR may apply to a New Zealand organisation that has an EU establishment, offers goods or services to people in the EU, or monitors their behaviour.
  • Māori data sovereignty emphasises authority, self-determination, consultation, participation, protection, and meaningful community agency.
  • Deficit framing should be limited when processing or interpreting Māori data.
  • Corporate governance concerns how a company is directed, controlled, overseen, and held accountable.
  • Sole traders and partnerships generally expose individuals to personal business liabilities, whereas a limited liability company is a separate legal entity whose shareholders are generally liable only up to their investment.
  • A trading name is distinct from the company’s legal entity name and should not normally be used as the contracting party.
  • Directors may delegate tasks, but delegation does not remove their ultimate governance responsibility.
  • Boards combine executive, non-executive, independent, technical, financial, legal, health-and-safety, sustainability, and industry perspectives.
  • Parent companies, subsidiaries, business units, franchises, vertical integration, and tiered supply chains affect accountability, risk, cost, and value distribution.
  • Strategy is a continuing cycle of strategic analysis, strategic direction, planning and implementation, and monitoring and evaluation.
  • Value added is created when a production stage transforms intermediate inputs into outputs of greater economic value.
  • GDP can be calculated by adding value added across production stages rather than counting every transaction price.

03 · WORK THE MECHANICS

Equations and worked patterns

  • Lecture 9 presented no mathematical equations.
  • Value added:

Value added = value of output − value of intermediate inputs

  • GDP using the value-added method:

GDP = sum of value added across relevant production stages

  • Simplified bread example from Lecture 10:
  • Farming: $1,200 − $700 = $500 value added.
  • Milling: $1,800 − $1,200 = $600 value added, using the lecture’s implied intermediate output value.
  • Bread production: $2,600 − $1,800 = $800 value added, using the same implied intermediate value.
  • Total value added: $500 + $600 + $800 = $1,900.
  • The intermediate flour value of $1,800 was inferred from the stated figures and was not presented as a complete explicit transaction table. Treat the worked example as a simplified classroom calculation.
  • The strategic improvement pattern is iterative: analyse the current position, set direction, plan and implement actions, monitor results, adjust actions, and revisit the analysis.
