02 · BUILD THE MODEL

Main concepts

  • Data privacy concerns the protection of personal information and individuals’ rights over its collection, storage, access, correction, use, transfer, and disclosure.
  • Data ethics evaluates how data is collected, interpreted, used, shared, protected, and applied to decisions.
  • Privacy and security support trust and an organisation’s social licence to operate.
  • Personal-data benefits, such as efficiency, personalisation, predictive accuracy, and improved services, must be balanced against risks to privacy, security, fairness, justice, transparency, and autonomy.
  • Engineers should design security into systems. The Optus example demonstrated the danger of APIs that allow record enumeration without effective authentication or object-level authorisation.
  • NDAs define confidential information, permitted uses and disclosures, protection requirements, duration, and consequences of breach. Access to information does not automatically provide permission to copy, transfer, publish, or reuse it.
  • Confidentiality obligations may arise from professional codes even when no NDA exists. Potential disclosures involving legal requirements, court orders, or significant harm require careful review and legal or professional advice.
  • The New Zealand Privacy Act 2020 was discussed in relation to collection purpose, notice, security, access, correction, retention, disclosure, offshore transfers, and breach reporting. The summary cautions that statutory details should be checked against authoritative sources.
  • The AAMI driver-monitoring example showed why engineers and users must distinguish technical capability from disclosed and contractually permitted uses.
  • GDPR obligations may apply to a New Zealand company that has an EU establishment, offers goods or services to people in the EU, or monitors their behaviour.
  • Māori data sovereignty emphasises self-determination, meaningful consultation, agency, participation, safeguards, and protection of collective interests.
  • Data-ethics analysis should consider privacy, transparency, bias, discrimination, and accountability alongside virtue ethics, consequentialism, deontology, and professional codes.
  • Engineers should question their own assumptions, challenge guru bias, speak up about ethical problems, seek diverse perspectives, and obtain help when working outside their competence.

03 · WORK THE MECHANICS

Equations and worked patterns

  • No mathematical equations were presented.
  • Privacy was summarised using four underlying principles:
  • Ownership
  • Access
  • Control
  • Possession
  • Worked analysis pattern for a data-driven system:
  1. Identify what data is collected.
  2. Establish why it is collected and whether the purpose is clearly disclosed.
  3. Check consent, access, correction, retention, security, and deletion arrangements.
  4. Identify secondary uses, recipients, jurisdictions, and possible law-enforcement disclosures.
  5. Assess risks involving privacy, fairness, bias, discrimination, autonomy, and accountability.
  6. Consider applicable legal, contractual, professional, and cultural obligations.
  7. Determine whether the safeguards and controls are reasonably practicable.
