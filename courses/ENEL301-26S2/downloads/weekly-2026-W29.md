## Main concepts

- Professional engineering requires more than technical correctness. Engineers must consider project viability, financial risk, stakeholders, teamwork, and social, environmental, legal, and ethical consequences.
- Legal compliance establishes a minimum boundary but does not by itself provide complete ethical justification.
- Three ethical approaches were introduced:
  1. **Deontological reasoning:** evaluate duties, rules, laws, and professional codes.
  2. **Consequentialist reasoning:** compare likely outcomes and overall benefits and harms, including serious harm imposed on minorities.
  3. **Virtue ethics:** consider what an honest, courageous, compassionate, and professionally responsible engineer would do.
- A defensible ethical decision should identify affected parties, examine duties and codes, compare consequences, and consider the professional values expressed by the decision.
- **Therac-25** illustrates that software behaviour, interfaces, testing, and safety controls can directly cause physical harm.
- **Boeing 737 MAX** illustrates how technical failures can interact with organisational culture and commercial pressure.
- Communication, collaboration, organisational culture, and stakeholder coordination can determine whether technically sound work succeeds.
- Interactive tutorials and workshops are part of the intended learning process and are not replaced by recordings.

## Equations and worked patterns

No equations were presented in the available summary.

A reusable ethical-analysis pattern is:

1. Define the engineering decision or failure.
2. Identify affected stakeholders.
3. Identify applicable duties, laws, codes, and professional responsibilities.
4. Compare the likely benefits and harms.
5. Consider which professional virtues the available choices demonstrate or violate.
6. Identify technical and organisational contributors.
7. Justify a decision using evidence from all three ethical approaches rather than relying on one framework alone.

For failure cases such as Therac-25 or Boeing 737 MAX, analyse:

- Technical failure modes
- Organisational contributors
- Affected stakeholders
- Neglected professional duties
- Potential safeguards or mitigations
