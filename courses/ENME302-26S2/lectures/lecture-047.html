Advantages of non-dimensionalisation

The lecture identified two major motivations:

1. Generalisation across physical scales and geometries

A dimensionless formulation can describe a broad family of cases rather than one specific combination of dimensional parameters. In fluid flow, cases with different characteristic lengths, velocities, densities, or viscosities can be compared using the same dimensionless parameter.

2. Reduction of the number of independent parameters

After substituting characteristic scales into the governing equations, several dimensional parameters combine into dimensionless groups. The resulting equation may depend on a smaller number of parameters, such as the Reynolds number, instead of separately depending on every original dimensional quantity.

The lecturer used flow around a cylinder as an example. The stated characteristic quantities included:

• Cylinder diameter \(D\).

• Characteristic flow velocity \(V_0\).

• Characteristic pressure \(P_0\).

The dimensionless coordinates and variables were represented using tildes, such as \(\tilde{x}\), \(\tilde{y}\), \(\tilde{u}\), \(\tilde{v}\), and \(\tilde{p}\).

Advantages and disadvantages of numerical models

Advantages discussed:

• Numerical methods can solve a wider range of problems than many analytical methods.

• Analytical solutions can be strongly constrained by geometry, boundary conditions, and the form of the governing equation.

• Numerical models can be used for complicated geometries and equations for which separation of variables is not practical.

• Numerical solutions can be relatively low-cost compared with physical experiments, depending on computational requirements.

• Numerical studies may be faster than constructing and running experiments.

• Numerical models allow investigation of dangerous, inaccessible, expensive, or difficult-to-repeat situations.

• Examples included earthquakes and nuclear-related phenomena, where direct experimentation may be impractical or unsafe.

• Numerical calculations can be used to compare or supplement analytical solutions.

Disadvantages discussed:

• A numerical model must be set up correctly.

• The correct governing physics and equations must be represented.

• If the model solves the wrong physics, its numerical accuracy does not make the result useful.

• Computational cost and solution time can sometimes make numerical modelling less attractive than a simple physical test.

• A cooking example was used to illustrate that physically testing a chicken patty could sometimes be quicker than developing and running a model.
