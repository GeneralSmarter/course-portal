The conceptual workflow is:

1. Formulate the governing PDE.

2. Discretize the PDE.

3. Solve the resulting algebraic system.

Forward-in-time, centred-in-space scheme

The forward-in-time, centred-in-space scheme uses:

• A forward difference for the time derivative.

• A centred difference for the second spatial derivative.

The time derivative is approximated by:

∂T/∂t ≈ (T_i^(n+1) - T_i^n)/Δt

The spatial second derivative is approximated by:

∂²T/∂x² ≈ (T_(i+1)^n - 2T_i^n + T_(i-1)^n)/(Δx)^2

All spatial values on the right-hand side are evaluated at the previous time level n, making the scheme explicit.

Substitution into the heat equation gives:

(T_i^(n+1) - T_i^n)/Δt - α [T_(i+1)^n - 2T_i^n + T_(i-1)^n]/(Δx)^2 = 0

The consistency analysis substitutes the exact continuous solution into the discrete equation. The residual is called the truncation error.

Taylor expansions are made about (x_i,t^n):

T(x_i,t^(n+1)) = T + Δt T_t + (Δt)^2/2 T_tt + higher-order terms

T(x_(i+1),t^n) = T + Δx T_x + (Δx)^2/2 T_xx + (Δx)^3/6 T_xxx + higher-order terms

T(x_(i-1),t^n) = T - Δx T_x + (Δx)^2/2 T_xx - (Δx)^3/6 T_xxx + higher-order terms

When the spatial terms are combined:

• The zeroth-order temperature terms cancel.

• The first spatial derivative terms cancel.

• The second spatial derivative terms combine to produce the desired central-difference approximation.

• The remaining spatial truncation contribution is of order (Δx)^2 after division by (Δx)^2.

• The forward time difference leaves a truncation contribution of order Δt.

The truncation error therefore has the form:

τ = O(Δt) + O((Δx)^2)

The scheme is consistent because:

τ → 0 as Δt → 0 and Δx → 0

The order of accuracy is:

• First order in time.

• Second order in space.

The lecture described this as unconditional consistency, meaning that the truncation error tends to zero as both step sizes tend to zero. This statement concerns consistency only. The explicit scheme still has a stability restriction on its time step.

Du Fort–Frankel scheme

The Du Fort–Frankel scheme was introduced as another finite-difference stencil and as an example requiring further analysis because it is not straightforwardly consistent under the usual limiting argument.

The time derivative uses a centred difference involving two time levels:

(T_i^(n+1) - T_i^(n-1))/(2Δt)

The spatial term has a modified midpoint structure. Instead of using 2T_i^n in the central spatial stencil, the scheme uses values at the forward and backward time levels, approximately of the form:

T_(i+1)^n - T_i^(n+1) - T_i^(n-1) + T_(i-1)^n

divided by (Δx)^2, multiplied by α.

T(1,t) = 0

Time derivative:

∂T/∂t ≈ (T_i^(n+1) - T_i^n)/Δt

Second spatial derivative:

∂²T/∂x² ≈ (T_(i+1)^n - 2T_i^n + T_(i-1)^n)/(Δx)^2

Combined residual equation:

(T_i^(n+1) - T_i^n)/Δt - α [T_(i+1)^n - 2T_i^n + T_(i-1)^n]/(Δx)^2 = 0

Truncation error

The truncation error is the residual obtained when the exact continuous solution is substituted into the discretized equation.

For the forward-in-time, centred-in-space scheme:

τ = O(Δt) + O((Δx)^2)

Thus the scheme is:

• First-order accurate in time.

• Second-order accurate in space.

• Consistent as Δt → 0 and Δx → 0.

Error definitions

Model error:

Error caused by simplifications or by the computational model failing to represent the physical system or geometry exactly.

Discretization error:

Difference between the exact solution of the continuous PDE and the exact solution of the discretized equations.

Solution error:

Difference between the exact discrete solution and the computed numerical solution.

Total error:

Difference between the exact continuous solution and the final computed solution.

Consistency:

A discretization is consistent if its truncation error approaches zero as the spatial and temporal step sizes approach zero.

Stability:

A numerical method is stable if numerical errors do not grow without bound during the computation. For the explicit heat-equation scheme, the lecture noted that an excessively large time-step parameter λ causes instability.

Convergence:

The numerical solution approaches the exact solution as the spatial and temporal discretizations are refined. The lecture presented consistency, stability, and convergence as related but distinct properties.
