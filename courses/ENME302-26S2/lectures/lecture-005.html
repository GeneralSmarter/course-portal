To model large deflections, the lecturer describes an iterative procedure:

2. Build the stiffness matrix using that geometry.

3. Solve for the displacement.

6. Repeat the solution and update process.

7. Continue until the solution converges.

Commercial finite-element packages may provide a large-deflection analysis option that performs this type of geometry update and iteration.

10. Degrees of freedom

The lecture distinguishes between:

• Element-level degrees of freedom.

• Structural-level, or global, degrees of freedom.

At the element level, the degrees of freedom describe the movement of each element node along the element’s local axis.

At the structural level, a degree of freedom is assigned wherever a node has the potential for non-zero displacement in a particular direction.

Important rules:

• A fully fixed or completely constrained node has no displacement degree of freedom in the constrained direction.

• A support location may still require a degree of freedom if movement is possible in one or more directions.

• The decision is based on the structure and its possible movement, not only on the particular loading case being analysed.

• The structural stiffness matrix is derived independently of the applied load.

• Applied loads are introduced after the structural degrees of freedom and stiffness relationships have been defined.

• Each displacement degree of freedom is paired with a corresponding applied-force component at the same location and in the same direction.

The lecturer strongly emphasises that assigning the structural degrees of freedom is an important early step. An incorrect degree-of-freedom numbering or assignment cannot generally be repaired by later calculations. This decision is expected to be made independently in an assessment.
