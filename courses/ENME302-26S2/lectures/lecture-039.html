Approximate temperature field

The exact temperature field is replaced by an approximate field, denoted \(\widetilde{T}\). For a two-node linear element,

\[

\widetilde{T}(x)

=

N_1(x)T_1+N_2(x)T_2.

\]

For an element spanning \(x_1\) to \(x_2\), the linear shape functions are

\[

N_1(x)=\frac{x_2-x}{x_2-x_1},

\]

\[

N_2(x)=\frac{x-x_1}{x_2-x_1}.

\]

Their endpoint values are

\[

N_1(x_1)=1,\qquad N_1(x_2)=0,

\]

\[

N_2(x_1)=0,\qquad N_2(x_2)=1.

\]

The derivatives are constant within the element:

\[

\frac{dN_1}{dx}

=

-\frac{1}{x_2-x_1},

\]

\[

\frac{dN_2}{dx}

=

\frac{1}{x_2-x_1}.

\]

Because the approximate temperature field is linear within each element,

\[

\frac{d\widetilde{T}}{dx}

=

\frac{T_2-T_1}{x_2-x_1},

\]

and its second derivative is zero inside the element:

\[

\frac{d^2\widetilde{T}}{dx^2}=0.

\]

The zero second derivative is one reason the strong-form equation is not applied directly to a piecewise-linear approximation. Instead, the weak form is obtained by integration by parts.

Residual and weighted residual method

Substituting the approximate field into the governing equation does not generally give zero. The remaining error is the residual:

\[

R(x)

=

\frac{d^2\widetilde{T}}{dx^2}+F(x).

\]

The weighted residual method requires the residual to be orthogonal to a set of weighting functions:

\[

\int_{\Omega}W_i(x)R(x)\,dx=0.

\]

The lecture used the finite-element interpolation functions as the weighting functions:

\[

W_i=N_i.

\]

This choice is the Galerkin method:

\[

\int_{\Omega}N_i(x)R(x)\,dx=0.

\]

The procedure is applied for each degree of freedom or each shape function. For a two-node element, there are two equations, corresponding to \(i=1\) and \(i=2\).

\[

\int_{x_1}^{x_2}

N_i

\frac{d^2\widetilde{T}}{dx^2}

\,dx.

\[

\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du.

u=N_i,

dv=\frac{d^2\widetilde{T}}{dx^2}\,dx,

\int_{x_1}^{x_2}

N_i

\frac{d^2\widetilde{T}}{dx^2}

N_i\frac{d\widetilde{T}}{dx}

For \(i=1\), the boundary contribution is

-\left.\frac{d\widetilde{T}}{dx}\right|_{x_1},

\left.\frac{d\widetilde{T}}{dx}\right|_{x_2},

The remaining integral produces the element stiffness contribution, while the source term produces the element forcing contribution.
